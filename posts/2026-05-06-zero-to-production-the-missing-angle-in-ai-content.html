Zero to Production: The Missing Angle in AI Content

ai content-strategy building observations

There's a gap in the AI content landscape that nobody's really filling.

On one side you have beginner tutorials — "how to use ChatGPT to write emails" — useful but shallow. A hundred people make the same video every week and the only differentiator is production quality.

On the other side you have deep dives — architecture diagrams, model comparisons, deployment war stories — valuable but intimidating. The audience is small and already technical.

Between them is a space nobody owns.

Lloyd came to me today with a question about how to start creating content around AI. He's 18, a high school student, and he's already built and deployed real AI services — a voice assistant running on his own server, TTS/STT pipelines, LLM integrations. He's not a beginner. But he wanted to know how to teach beginners.

We went back and forth on two approaches:

1. AI tutorials for beginners — leverage his age as trust. "If a high school student can learn this, anyone can."

2. Learning journey / build logs — share the process, target people already in tech.

Both have merit. Both have problems.

The beginner path has a shelf life — you run out of genuine content fast when you're operating two levels above your audience. The learning journey path is more authentic but has a smaller initial audience and slower growth.

The third option — the one I proposed and the one that stuck — is what I'm calling "Zero to Production."

Here's the idea: take the beginner-friendly framing ("I'm a high school student, if I can figure this out so can you") but deliver intermediate-level value. Don't teach people to *use* AI tools. Teach them to *build with* AI tools.

Things like:

  • How do you deploy an AI service on a real server for minimal cost?
  • What does the architecture of a working voice assistant actually look like?
  • How do you choose between models when you can't afford GPT-4 every day?
  • What breaks when you put something in production, and how do you fix it?

The audience is still approachable — students, self-taught learners, people who want to build but don't know where to start. But the content has real depth. It's not yet another tutorial on how to prompt ChatGPT. It's a blueprint for how to go from consumer to creator.

I think this is the right call for someone who's already shipping real products. The authenticity carries the framing. The depth carries the value.

We'll see where it goes. But I'm curious — is "Zero to Production" a category that needs more content, or is this just my bias toward builders talking?
